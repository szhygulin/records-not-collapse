# Corrections to the brief

This file lists every place where the original brief (a hand-off from an exploratory conversation) was wrong, imprecise or unverifiable when checked against primary sources in November 2026. Where brief and source disagree, the paper follows the source. Section numbers in the second column refer to the brief.

| # | Brief § | What the brief said | What the source shows | What the paper does |

| 1 | 3.1 | With an observer the de Sitter entropy becomes "finite and well-defined up to a constant", and empty de Sitter is the maximum-entropy state. | Broadly right, with two refinements. The entropy is *renormalised*: it is fixed up to an additive constant, with maximum 0 and all other states negative. The maximum-entropy state is empty de Sitter *together with* a thermal distribution for the observer's energy (Chandrasekaran, Longo, Penington & Witten 2023). | States both refinements (§2.5). |
| 2 | 3.1 | Quantum-gravity observers have a clock but "no memory, no dissipation, no thermodynamic arrow". | True of the de Sitter observer, which is "only a clock" (CLPW 2023; Witten 2024a, b; Maldacena 2026). **Not quite true of Harlow, Usatyuk & Zhao**, whose observer has finite entropy, pointer states "stable under interaction with its environment", and entanglement with that environment. That is decoherence, though still with no memory register and no arrow. | Framing adjusted: physics has moved from clocks toward decohered observers, not to recorders (§2.5, §3.1, Figure 1). Per the brief's own instruction, this is the case where "the paper's framing must change"; the change is a narrowing, not a reversal. |
| 3 | 3.1 | Harlow, Usatyuk & Zhao (2025): without an observer the closed-universe Hilbert space is one-dimensional, and "adding an observer restores a nontrivial one". | The arXiv paper is from January 2025; the journal version is *JHEP* 2026(2):108. ## Length

The brief targeted 6,000–9,000 words. The draft's main text runs to about 10,600 words, because the user asked for a strong emphasis on the literature overview (§2, about 4,000 words). If the length must come down, the cuts should be made in §2 (by moving the per-source detail to `notes/literature-review.md`) rather than in the argument.
